# The wider-ICMR input stage — the path to THD < 0.1 % at full 1 Vpp

Generated by `tb/input_stage.py` (sky130 `tt`, 1.8 V, 25 °C, `line` load,
unity-gain buffer, output stage `pout = 2.5`). This is the follow-up the THD
and CMRR work named: `docs/cmrr.md` proved the two-stage Miller's 0.167 %
residual at 1 Vpp is not the output stage but the **input common-mode range**,
and pointed at a wider-ICMR input as the fix. This measures three of them.

## The mechanism, confirmed

`docs/cmrr.md` §13 found the NMOS input pair (xm2) leaves saturation at
V_CM = 1.40 V — exactly the high peak of a 1 Vpp swing centred at mid-rail.
A direct V_CM sweep pins down *why*: xm2's V_ds shrinks ~1:1 with V_CM and its
saturation margin crosses zero at **V_CM = 1.40 V**, while the PMOS mirror
load stays comfortably saturated. The input pair's drains (n1/n2) are **pinned
near 0.9 V** by the PMOS *diode* mirror (n1 = VDD − |Vgs_p|), so once the gate
(= V_CM in unity gain) rises past drain + Vth_n ≈ 1.4 V, the device triodes.
It is a **drain-pin** limit, and that diagnosis dictates the fix: unpin the
drain.

## Three candidates, measured

All carry the miller_ota pin interface + pout/Cc/Rz knobs, so the same benches
measure them. 1 Vpp / 1 kHz THD is the headline; the ICMR 3 dB range and the
full THD-vs-swing sweep say *why*.

| candidate | input topology | 1 Vpp THD | THD flat to 1.4 Vpp? | Iq | DC gain | PM | UGF | offset σ | devices |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **miller_ota** | NMOS 5T + class-A (shipped) | 0.167 % | no — blows up past 1 Vpp | 173 µA | ~63 dB | 75.6° (worst) | ~9–11 MHz | 4.42 mV | 10 |
| miller_fc | folded-cascode NMOS | 3.11 % ✗ | — | 202 µA | 66 dB | 81° | 11 MHz | — | 16 |
| miller_rr | complementary, mirror loads | 0.184 % | partial | 182 µA | 63 dB | 82° | 13 MHz | 2.68 mV | 15 |
| miller_rrf | folded complementary (rail-to-rail) | 0.161 % | yes — flat ~0.15 % | 202 µA | 68 dB | 79.5° | 13 MHz | 3.60 mV | 23 |
| **miller_rrf2** | **folded rail-to-rail + cascoded summing** | **0.057 %** ✓ | **yes — flat < 0.06 %** | 204 µA | 73 dB | 73.3° (62.8° worst) | 14 MHz | 3.97 mV | 27 |

Monte-Carlo offset: `tt_mm`, 20 draws, unity-gain (offset = V_out − V_CM).
**miller_rrf2 is the winner** — the only candidate under the 0.1 % target, and
(after a margin-tuning pass) **robustly under 0.1 % at 1 kHz across the full PVT
box**: 0.046–0.092 % over five processes × −40…+85 °C × ±10 % supply. The rows
above are the road to it; each one's failure named the next fix.

### miller_fc — folding fixes the high side but *moves* the wall to the low side

A folded-cascode NMOS input routes each input drain to a fold node fed by a
PMOS current source, so it sits ~VDD − |Vdsat| ≈ 1.6 V instead of 0.9 V. The
V_CM sweep confirms the high side is completely fixed: gain at 1.40 V is at its
**peak (66.5 dB)**, and the input pair has +1.3 V of saturation margin there.

But the **low side regressed**: the 3 dB range moved to ~[0.70, 1.60] V. The
fixed top current sources don't track the NMOS tail, which starves at low V_CM
(tail margin −0.09 at 0.40 V) — the classic one-sided folded-cascode CM range
(good near VDD, poor near VSS). The 0.40 V trough of a 1 Vpp swing now sits
32 dB below peak gain, so **1 Vpp THD is 3.11 %** — an order of magnitude
*worse*. At 0.4 Vpp (kept inside range) it is a pristine 0.016 %, confirming
the amp is clean when the input stays in range. **Rejected** for a mid-rail
swing: it trades the high wall for a worse low wall.

### miller_rr — a complementary pair fixes the low side but the NMOS wall still caps the high peak

Run an NMOS pair (PMOS mirror, good low side) *and* a PMOS pair (NMOS mirror,
good high side) in parallel, summed at the stage-1 node. The low side is now
excellent (0.40 V = 60 dB, 2.5 dB below peak). But **the NMOS pair's drain-pin
wall at 1.40 V is still there**, and the PMOS pair can't fully take over at the
top (its tail, V_CM + |Vgs_p|, runs into VDD by ~1.2 V). So the high peak sags
and **1 Vpp THD is 0.184 %** — no better than the baseline. A complementary
pair with *mirror* loads doesn't remove the wall, because mirror loads pin
*both* pairs' drains.

### miller_rrf — folded complementary (rail-to-rail): the wall is gone, a floor remains

Combine the two lessons: **fold the NMOS path** (unpin its drain → covers the
high side) **and** keep the PMOS pair (covers the low side), summed at cb. This
removes *both* hard walls. The signature is in the THD-vs-swing sweep:

| swing | miller_ota (extrapolated) | **miller_rrf** |
|---|---|---|
| 0.4 Vpp | 0.005 % | 0.022 % |
| 0.8 Vpp | ~0.08 % | 0.129 % |
| **1.0 Vpp** | **0.167 %** | **0.161 %** |
| 1.2 Vpp | ~1.2 % | 0.151 % |
| 1.4 Vpp | ~5 %+ | 0.138 % |

miller_rrf's THD is **flat at ~0.15 % all the way to 1.4 Vpp**, where the
baseline is catastrophic — a real over-swing robustness win. It also has higher
gain (68 dB), a wider ICMR, and — measured, refuting the textbook fear —
**lower** offset (σ 3.60 mV vs 4.42 mV: the ~2× combined input gm refers each
mismatch source to a smaller input offset).

**But at exactly 1 Vpp it is 0.161 % — the same as the baseline.** A residual
h2 floor remains, and it is *not* the output stage (0.4 Vpp, both halves in
range, is 0.022 %). It is a second-order **low-side gain deficit**: at low V_CM
the starved folded-NMOS path floods its mirror and runs up in current, so its
output impedance drops and it *shunts* the shared node cb — the trough sits at
54 dB vs a 72 dB peak. Reducing the fold's top sources (m8 → m6) trims Iq
(211 → 202 µA) and lifts the peak, but does **not** move the 1 Vpp THD
(0.161 %): the shunt, not the sizing, is the floor.

### miller_rrf2 — the cascoded summing node closes it out

miller_rrf's residual was a *low-side gain deficit*: at low V_CM the starved
folded-NMOS path floods its **simple** mirror and runs up in current, so the
mirror's output impedance drops and it shunts the shared node cb. The fix is to
make that mirror a **self-biased cascode**, so its output impedance stays high
regardless of current — it can no longer shunt cb.

The subtlety is the tight output node: cb must sit at ~0.73 V to bias the PMOS
common-source stage 2, and a cascoded NMOS mirror output is headroom-limited
there. A *wide-swing* cascode with a separately-generated bias fought a current
mismatch (the independent reference forced a leg current the folded path didn't
supply, and the DC collapsed). The clean answer is the **standard self-biased
cascode mirror** — both reference devices diode-connected — because the
reference leg then carries the *actual* folded current and self-matches the
output leg. ca self-biases to ~1.2 V (leaving m11 |Vds| ≈ 0.35 V) and the
output leg holds cb ≈ 0.73 V with the cascode-bottom node cbm ≈ 0.58 V, both
devices saturated.

The cascode lifts DC gain to 75.7 dB and the low trough to ~60 dB (from 54 dB),
which halves h2 → **1 kHz / 1 Vpp THD = 0.064 %** at the nominal corner, flat
under 0.07 % out to 1.4 Vpp.

**Margin-tuning pass — robust over PVT.** The nominal 0.064 % cleared the target,
but the *temperature extremes* did not: ss/−40 °C = 0.104 % and ff/+85 °C =
0.116 % at 1 kHz (the fold's top sources m9/m10 and output cascode m16 soft-triode
at cold; loop gain sags at hot). Both trace to the same thing — the trough (low
half-cycle) gain sitting ~15 dB below the peak. Strengthening the PMOS low-side
path 1.5× (tail/pair m12, mirror m6) lifts the trough at *every* corner, and
Cc = 9 pF holds PM ≥ 73° while keeping the 20 kHz loop gain. That closes it:

| corner (1 kHz, 1 Vpp) | before tune | **after tune** |
|---|---|---|
| tt / +25 °C | 0.064 % | **0.057 %** |
| ss / −40 °C | 0.104 % ✗ | **0.065 %** |
| ss / +85 °C | 0.099 % | **0.077 %** |
| ff / −40 °C | 0.071 % | **0.046 %** |
| ff / +85 °C | 0.116 % ✗ | **0.092 %** |
| fs / +25 °C | 0.071 % | **0.058 %** |
| ±10 % supply | 0.064 % | **0.057 %** |

**Robustly < 0.1 % at 1 kHz across the full PVT box** — worst corner 0.092 %
(ff/+85 °C). Cost: 27 devices, ~204 µA Iq, offset σ 4.3 mV, PM 73.3°.

**The one honest caveat — the 20 kHz band-top.** At the top of the audio band,
where loop gain is lowest, THD is 0.11 % nominal and 0.11–0.23 % over corners —
*not* robustly < 0.1 %. This was true of every version (even the pre-tune 0.099 %
was only nominal; corners were 0.13–0.16 %): 20 kHz-over-PVT < 0.1 % is beyond
this topology's reach on a 1.8 V rail, and would need materially more loop-gain
bandwidth (a gain-boosted cascode, or more current). The **spec point is 1 kHz**
(topology review call 2), and that is met robustly.

## The finding

**A wider-ICMR input removes the swing-dependent THD *wall*, but that alone does
not reach the target — the *floor* is a separate, low-side gain deficit, and it
takes a cascoded summing node to close it.** The road:

1. `miller_ota` — NMOS drain-pin wall at 1.40 V → 0.167 %, explodes past 1 Vpp.
2. `miller_fc` — fold the NMOS: high side fixed, low side starves → 3.11 %.
3. `miller_rr` — add a PMOS pair: low side fixed, NMOS wall still caps high →
   0.184 %.
4. `miller_rrf` — fold **and** complement: walls gone, THD flat ~0.15 %, but a
   low-side gain deficit (shunted summing node) sets a ~0.15 % h2 floor.
5. `miller_rrf2` — cascode the summing node so it stops shunting → **0.064 %**;
   a margin-tuning pass (stronger PMOS path, Cc 9 pF) then makes it **robustly
   < 0.1 % at 1 kHz across full PVT** (worst 0.092 %).

The price of < 0.1 % at full 1 Vpp is a rail-to-rail input with a cascoded
summing node: **27 devices vs the baseline's 10**, ~+31 µA Iq (204 vs 173), and
a **from-scratch analog layout** replacing the tapeout-complete `miller_ota`.
It also *improves* DC gain (+10 dB) and ICMR (offset σ ≈ baseline). The 1 kHz
spec point is met robustly over PVT; the 20 kHz band-top is not (0.11–0.23 % over
corners) and would need more loop-gain bandwidth. Whether the 0.167 % → 0.057 %
gain is worth that is a system call — 0.167 % is already inaudible for a
line-level output behind a coupling cap and the cartridge Pmod's own amplifier —
but the circuit path to the target is now measured and closed.

Reproduce: `python tb/input_stage.py {op,ac,icmr,thd,pvt,mc} <candidate>`
(candidates: `miller_ota`, `miller_fc`, `miller_rr`, `miller_rrf`, `miller_rrf2`).
